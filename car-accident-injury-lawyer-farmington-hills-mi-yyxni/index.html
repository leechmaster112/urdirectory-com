Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Farmington Hills Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system. In most cases, your own auto policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is supposed to pay your crash-related medical bills and a portion of your wage loss regardless of who caused the crash. But major reforms that took effect in 2020 changed how this works for Farmington Hills drivers: Drivers now choose a PIP medical coverage level—for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, or in some cases $50,000 or an opt-out—instead of everyone automatically getting unlimited lifetime medical benefits. A statewide fee schedule and cost controls limit what many medical providers can charge for auto-related care, which can affect your choice of doctors and the types of services available. Once your chosen PIP limit is reached, you may be forced to rely on health insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, or out-of-pocket funds for future care. On top of your PIP/no-fault claim, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” standard (serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death). That claim can include pain and suffering and other losses that PIP never pays for.
