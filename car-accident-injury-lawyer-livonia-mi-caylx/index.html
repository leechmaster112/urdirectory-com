Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Livonia Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system, which generally means your own policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is responsible for paying your accident-related medical bills and certain wage-loss benefits regardless of who caused the crash. But major no-fault reforms that took effect in 2020 changed how that looks in real life: Drivers can now choose among several PIP medical coverage levels (for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, and in limited cases $50,000 or opt-outs), instead of being automatically locked into unlimited lifetime medical care. A new medical fee schedule limits what many providers can charge for auto-related treatment, which can affect what care is available and how bills get paid. If your PIP medical limit is lower, you may need to lean more heavily on health insurance or other coverage once that limit is reached. On top of PIP/no-fault benefits, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering and other damages if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” requirement (serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death).
