Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Macomb Township Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system, which means your own policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is usually responsible for paying your accident-related medical bills and certain wage-loss benefits—no matter who caused the crash. But major no-fault reforms that took effect in 2020 changed how this works in important ways: Drivers now choose from multiple PIP medical coverage levels (for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, or in some cases $50,000 or an opt-out), instead of everyone automatically having unlimited lifetime medical coverage. New fee schedules and cost controls limit what many medical providers can charge for auto-related treatment, which can affect access to certain services and specialists. When PIP limits are lower, injured people may need to rely more heavily on health insurance or other coverage once PIP benefits are exhausted. On top of your PIP/no-fault benefits, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” standard (serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death). That claim can include compensation for pain and suffering and other losses that PIP does not pay.
