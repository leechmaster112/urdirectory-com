Frequently Asked Questions

  • What makes a workers' compensation attorney the "best"?
  • The best workers' compensation attorneys possess extensive experience in this specific area of law, a proven track record of successful case outcomes, strong negotiation and litigation skills, excellent communication with clients, and a deep understanding of the relevant state and federal laws.
  • How do I find the best workers' compensation attorney near me?
  • To find the best workers' compensation attorney near you, research local bar associations, read online reviews, ask for referrals from trusted sources, and schedule initial consultations with several attorneys to assess their expertise and suitability for your case.
  • What is the typical fee structure for a workers' compensation lawyer?
  • Most workers' compensation attorneys work on a contingency fee basis, meaning they only get paid if they recover compensation for you. Their fee is typically a percentage of the settlement or award, usually between 20-33%.
  • When should I hire a workers' compensation attorney?
  • You should consider hiring a workers' compensation attorney as soon as possible after your injury, especially if your claim is denied, complex, or if you anticipate significant disputes with the insurance company.
  • Can I change my workers' compensation attorney if I'm not satisfied?
  • Yes, you generally have the right to change your workers' compensation attorney, though you will need to handle the transition carefully to ensure no interruption in your case and to address any fees owed to the previous attorney.
  • What questions should I ask a potential workers' compensation attorney?
  • Key questions include: How long have you practiced workers' compensation law? What percentage of your cases are workers' compensation claims? What is your experience with cases similar to mine? What are your fees?
  • What is the role of a workers' compensation attorney in my claim?
  • A workers' compensation attorney acts as your advocate, guiding you through the legal process, communicating with insurance adjusters and employers, gathering evidence, negotiating settlements, and representing you in court if necessary.
  • How does an attorney help if my workers' compensation claim is denied?
  • If your claim is denied, a workers' compensation attorney can help by investigating the reason for denial, gathering additional evidence, filing necessary appeals, and representing you in hearings to overturn the denial and secure your benefits.
  • Is it worth hiring a workers' compensation attorney for a minor injury?
  • For minor injuries, it may depend on the complexity of the claim and the responsiveness of the insurance company. However, an attorney can still be valuable to ensure you receive all entitled benefits and avoid future complications.
  • What is the difference between a personal injury attorney and a workers' compensation attorney?
  • While related, a workers' compensation attorney specializes in claims arising from on-the-job injuries under specific statutory schemes, whereas a personal injury attorney handles a broader range of injury cases, including those from accidents like car crashes or slip-and-falls.
