Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What is TMS therapy?
A: TMS, or Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation, is a non-invasive procedure that uses magnetic pulses to stimulate nerve cells in the brain, offering a potential treatment option for depression and other mood disorders.

Q: Is TMS therapy effective for depression?
A: TMS therapy has shown promising results in treating depression, especially for individuals who haven't found relief with antidepressants. Studies indicate that many patients experience significant improvements in their symptoms.

Q: What conditions can TMS therapy treat?
A: Besides depression, TMS therapy is being explored as a treatment for other conditions, including anxiety, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Check with providers like Onward Psychiatry for details.

Q: How long does a TMS therapy session last?
A: A typical TMS session lasts for about 20 to 60 minutes, depending on the treatment protocol.

Q: How many TMS sessions are typically needed?
A: A typical course of TMS therapy involves daily sessions, five times a week, for four to six weeks. The exact number of sessions and the overall treatment duration vary based on individual needs and response to treatment. Your doctor can help determine the best plan for you, just like the experts at Onward Psychiatry.

Q: What are the side effects of TMS therapy?
A: TMS therapy is generally well-tolerated. Common side effects may include mild scalp discomfort or headaches, which usually subside after a few sessions. More serious side effects are rare.

Q: How do I prepare for TMS therapy?
A: Before starting TMS therapy, you'll undergo an evaluation to determine if you're a good candidate. You may need to discontinue certain medications or undergo other assessments. Your provider will give you specific instructions, like Onward Psychiatry does for their patients, to follow before each session.

Q: What should I expect during a TMS session?
A: During a session, you'll sit in a comfortable chair while a trained technician positions the TMS coil near your head. The coil delivers magnetic pulses, which you may hear as clicking sounds. You'll remain awake and alert throughout this procedure.
