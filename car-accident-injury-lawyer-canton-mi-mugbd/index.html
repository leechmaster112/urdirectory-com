Michigan no-fault basics – and why a lawyer still matters in Canton Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system, which means your own policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage usually pays your accident-related medical bills and some wage-loss benefits regardless of who caused the crash. But recent no-fault reforms have made things more complicated for Canton drivers: You now choose a PIP medical coverage level (such as unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, or in some cases lower), instead of automatically having unlimited medical benefits. New fee schedules limit what many medical providers can charge for auto-related treatment. When PIP coverage runs out or a benefit is disputed, you may be pushed toward health insurance or left with uncovered bills. On top of that, if your injuries meet Michigan’s legal “threshold injury” standard (serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death), you may also have a claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering and other damages.
