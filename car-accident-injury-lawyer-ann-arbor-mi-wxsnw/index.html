Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Ann Arbor Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system, which means your own policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage typically pays your crash-related medical bills and some wage-loss benefits, no matter who caused the wreck. But after Michigan’s 2019–2020 auto insurance reforms, the fine print matters a lot more: Drivers can now choose among several PIP medical limits, from unlimited down to capped options (and, in some cases, even opt out), instead of everyone automatically having unlimited lifetime medical coverage. Insurers may challenge whether treatment is “reasonable and necessary” or try to limit or cut off benefits under the new fee schedules and utilization rules. If your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” standard (serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death), you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering and other damages.
