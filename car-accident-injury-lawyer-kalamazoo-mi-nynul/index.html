How Michigan No-Fault Works After a Kalamazoo Crash Michigan uses a “no-fault” auto insurance system, which means that after most crashes, your own auto policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is the first place to look for payment of medical bills and some wage loss, regardless of who caused the collision. Recent reforms allow drivers to choose different PIP medical limits—such as unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, or in some cases $50,000—each with different protections and risks. Because of these changes, many Kalamazoo drivers now have lower PIP limits than in the past, and insurance companies use that to minimize what they pay. When injuries are serious—such as fractures, surgeries, traumatic brain injuries, or long-term disability—you may also have a claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering, excess wage loss, and future medical expenses. This “third-party” claim is separate from your no-fault benefits and can be worth significantly more than the bills you see today. A knowledgeable Kalamazoo car accident attorney can review your coverage, the police report, and your medical records to identify every possible claim.
