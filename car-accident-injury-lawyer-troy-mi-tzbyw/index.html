Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Troy Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system. In simple terms, your own auto policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is usually responsible for paying your accident-related medical bills and certain wage-loss benefits—no matter who caused the crash. However, major no-fault reforms that took effect in 2020 changed how that really works for Troy drivers: You now choose among several PIP medical coverage levels (for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, and in some cases $50,000 or even an opt-out), instead of everyone automatically having unlimited lifetime medical coverage. A statewide medical fee schedule and cost controls limit what providers can charge for auto-related care, which can affect which services are available and how they’re billed. Drivers with lower PIP limits may have to lean more heavily on health insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, or pay out of pocket when PIP medical coverage is exhausted. On top of your PIP/no-fault benefits, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver for pain and suffering and other losses if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” standard (serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death).
