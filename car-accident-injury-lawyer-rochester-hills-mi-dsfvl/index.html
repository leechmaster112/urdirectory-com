Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Rochester Hills Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system. In most cases, your own auto policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is supposed to pay your crash-related medical bills and part of your wage loss regardless of who caused the crash. But major reforms that took effect starting in 2020 changed how this really works in everyday cases: You now choose from several PIP medical coverage levels—for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, or (in some situations) $50,000 or even a medical opt-out—instead of everyone automatically having unlimited lifetime benefits. A statewide medical fee schedule and cost controls limits what many providers can charge for auto-related care, which can affect which doctors you see and how your treatment is billed. If you chose a lower PIP limit, you may have to rely more on health insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, or out-of-pocket funds once that limit is reached. On top of your PIP/no-fault benefits, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” standard (serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death). That kind of claim can include pain and suffering and other losses that PIP never pays for.
