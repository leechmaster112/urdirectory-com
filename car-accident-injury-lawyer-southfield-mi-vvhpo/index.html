Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Southfield Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system. That means your own policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is usually responsible for paying crash-related medical bills and a portion of your wage loss—regardless of who caused the accident. However, major reforms that took effect in 2020 changed what that really looks like: Drivers now choose among several PIP medical coverage levels (for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, and in some cases $50,000 or even a medical opt-out), instead of everyone automatically having unlimited lifetime benefits. A statewide medical fee schedule and cost controls limits what many providers can charge for auto-related care, which can affect which doctors you see and how treatment is billed. If you selected a lower PIP limit, you may have to rely on health insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, or pay out of pocket once that coverage runs out. In addition to your PIP claim, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” standard—serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death. That third-party claim can include pain and suffering and other non-economic damages that PIP never covers.
