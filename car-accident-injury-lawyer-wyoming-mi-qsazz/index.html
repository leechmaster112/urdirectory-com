Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Wyoming Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system, which generally means your own policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage is responsible for paying your accident-related medical bills and a portion of your wage loss—regardless of who caused the crash. But major reforms that took effect in 2020 changed how that works: Michigan drivers now choose among multiple PIP medical coverage levels (for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, and in some cases $50,000 or a medical opt-out), instead of everyone automatically having unlimited lifetime medical benefits. A statewide medical fee schedule and cost controls limit what many providers can charge for auto-related care, which can affect which services are available and how they’re billed. People who chose lower PIP limits may have to lean more heavily on health insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, or out-of-pocket funds once PIP benefits run out. On top of PIP, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” standard—serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death. That third-party claim can cover pain and suffering and other non-economic damages that PIP never pays for.
