Michigan no-fault basics and why a lawyer still matters in Westland Michigan uses a no-fault auto insurance system. In simple terms, your own auto policy’s Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage usually pays for your crash-related medical bills and certain wage-loss benefits—regardless of who caused the crash. But recent no-fault reforms changed how that works in everyday cases: Drivers now choose among several PIP medical coverage levels (for example, unlimited, $500,000, $250,000, and in some situations $50,000 or even an opt-out), instead of everyone being locked into unlimited lifetime coverage. A statewide medical fee schedule and cost controls limit what providers can charge for auto-related care, which can affect which doctors you see and how bills are handled. If you chose a lower PIP limit, you may need to rely on health insurance, Medicare/Medicaid, or out-of-pocket funds once that limit is reached. On top of your own PIP benefits, you may also have a separate claim against the at-fault driver if your injuries meet Michigan’s “threshold injury” requirement—serious impairment of body function, permanent serious disfigurement, or death. That claim can cover pain and suffering and other non-economic damages that PIP never pays.
